## How the Trucking Insurance Calculator Works?

Our calculator considers key variables that directly impact your commercial truck insurance cost, including:

###### Truck Type

Semi trucks typically have higher premiums due to long-haul exposure, while straight trucks and hotshots may cost less depending on their use.

###### Garaging Location

Trucks based in urban areas face higher premiums due to traffic, theft, and claims frequency, while rural locations are lower risk.

###### Years in Business

New authorities generally face higher rates, while established businesses with a solid track record can secure better pricing.

###### Operating Radius

Local routes are typically cheaper, while regional and interstate (500+ miles) routes increase your trucking insurance cost.

###### Cargo Type

Standard freight costs less to insure, but high-risk or specialized cargo drives premiums higher.

###### Claims History

A clean driving record can help lower costs, while past claims raise your premium.

###### Driver Quality

Experienced drivers with clean records are less risky, lowering your rate.

###### OOS (Out-of-Service) Records

A higher percentage of out-of-service trucks can negatively affect your insurance pricing.

###### Credit Score

A better credit score can reduce your semi-truck insurance cost by up to 15%.

###### DOT & Safety Data

Insurers evaluate compliance and safety records to assess risk.

Note

This is a non-binding estimate. Final premiums are determined by underwriting. The result is a calculated annual estimate, giving you a practical range to understand your costs, plan your budget, and avoid surprises.

1. How much does trucking insurance cost per month?

Most owner-operators pay $700 to $1,300 per month for primary liability trucking insurance. The exact cost depends on factors such as truck type, driving record, cargo, operating radius, and years in business.

This trucking insurance calculator helps estimate your annual premium based on these factors before getting a full quote.

2. Is the trucking insurance calculator accurate?

The calculator provides a realistic estimate based on common pricing factors used in commercial truck insurance, including truck type, operating radius, cargo, and safety history.

However, it is not a final quote. Insurance companies determine the exact premium after reviewing detailed records during underwriting.

3. Why do new trucking companies pay higher insurance rates?

New authorities usually pay higher trucking insurance premiums because insurers have no operating history to evaluate risk. Without past safety records or claims data, carriers price policies more conservatively.

Rates often improve after 12–24 months of clean operation.
