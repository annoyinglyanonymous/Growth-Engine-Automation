## Understanding Insurance Claim Attorney Fees

Most insurance claim attorneys work on a contingency fee basis, meaning they only get paid if you recover money from your insurance company. Typical contingency fees range from 25% to 40% of the recovery amount, with the percentage often depending on:

- The complexity of your claim

- Whether litigation becomes necessary

- The stage at which your claim resolves

- The total recovery amount

Some firms also charge separate fees for case expenses like expert witnesses, court filing fees, and investigation costs. Before hiring any attorney, request a clear written explanation of their fee structure and potential additional costs.

## Signs Your Insurance Company Is Acting in Bad Faith

Understanding when an insurer crosses the line from tough negotiation to bad faith practices can help you determine when legal representation becomes necessary. Watch for these warning signs:

- Unreasonable Delays

While some delay in the investigative process may be normal, undue delay without adequate reason may demonstrate bad faith. Most states have established timelines insurers must comply with to acknowledge, investigate, and pay a claim. For example, Florida requires insurers acknowledge a claim within 14 days and to make a coverage decision within 90 days.

- Inadequate Investigation

Insurers are obligated to thoroughly investigate claims before making decisions about coverage. Some signs of willful blindness are adjusters who spend very short times looking at damage, adjusters or other staff members who refuse to acknowledge evidence you provided, adjusters using unqualified people to evaluate damage that requires a specialist, and adjusters making coverage decisions before they receive reports from experts.

- Misrepresenting Policy Provisions

Certain insurers try to deny legitimate claims by misreading the policy language or quoting exclusions that are really not applicable to the claim. This misreading is especially frustrating in complicated claims that have multiple possible causes of loss, when the insurer will sometimes try to blame the damage on an excluded peril, even when the evidence suggests that is it not an excluded peril.

- Lowball Settlement Offers

While negotiation and settlement offers are anticipated, offers that significantly undervalue your loss may signify bad faith, particularly when insurers cannot provide a basis for their valuations. Be quick to recognize estimates without the obvious and necessary damage items, without market prices for labor or materials, depreciate excessively, or ignores contractor estimates.

## The Claim Process: Working with an Attorney

Knowing how the usual process works with an attorney that specializes in insurance claims may help you to set realistic expectations. After your initial consultation, your attorney will most likely review your full insurance policy and claim files, speak to any relevant experts, look up information about similar claims, and give you their insights about the strength of your claim.

Before a lawsuit is filed, attorneys will typically send a formal demand letter to the insurance company, provide any additional supporting documentation and any expert reports, attend settlement conferences, and negotiate with the insurance company’s attorneys. Many claims are resolved during this stage in the process, particularly when attorneys send very thorough demands.

If pre-litigation efforts don’t produce a fair settlement, your attorney may file a formal lawsuit, conduct discovery, engage in motion practice, prepare expert witnesses, and represent you at trial if needed. After your claim resolves, your attorney will review settlement documents, address any liens, and disburse settlement funds after deducting agreed-upon fees.

## Document Everything: The Policyholder's Most Powerful Tool

Whether pursuing a claim on your own or with an attorney, accurate documents greatly increase your odds of success. Insurance claim attorneys often cite poor documentation as the primary reason why claims become disputed.

Create a complete chronological record of all conversations with your insurer; all inspections of the property; any promises by adjusters; all weather conditions pertaining to your property; and, the effects of all damage to your daily life and business.

High-quality photo and video documentation should include wide shots for overall context and close-ups for specific damage, include documentation of all damaged areas, include reference items for scale, document emergency repairs, be dated and organized, and document weather conditions as needed.

For large claims, you might consider securing independent damage assessments from licensed contractors, an engineering report for structural damage, an environmental testing report for contaminants, and a business income loss projection from accountants. Although these services require you to pay upfront, they often pay for themselves by potentially strengthening your claim position.

## Conclusion

When considering an attorney to help you in an insurance dispute, ensure that they have direct experience dealing with your type of claim. For example, an attorney experienced with hurricane damage and a property damage claim may not be the best attorney to pursue a business interruption claim or indemnity dispute, for instance. Asking for real examples of similar cases, and their resolution, is a good way to assess an attorney. You should consider issues like firm capacity as well. You want your attorney to have the resources to match the insurer’s team of adjusters and other experts, especially when dealing with complex claims and claims of a high monetary value.

Just as important is an attorney’s communicative ability. Insurance claims can take a long time to resolve if everything is done properly, which is why you want to deal with someone who will be responsive, transparent, and really care about the resolution of your case. Insurers rely on the facts that policyholders are uninformed or under duress. Having a strong legal advocate doesn’t fully level the playing field with your insurer, but it absolutely increases the likelihood of achieving a fair resolution.
