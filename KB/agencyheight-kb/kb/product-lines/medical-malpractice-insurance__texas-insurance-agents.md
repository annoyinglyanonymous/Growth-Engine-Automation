## Is Medical Malpractice Insurance Mandatory in Texas?

No, Medical Malpractice Insurance is not mandatory by law in the state of Texas, but it is recommended to get medical malpractice coverage to get covered against unexpected malpractice lawsuits.

In the case of private practitioners or contract-based workers that are not fully covered by the hospital’s medical liability, getting your own policy is important to get financially protected against personal liability for legal fees, settlements, or judgments.

Texas has a “statute of limitations” legislation that sets a two-year time restriction for a patient or their survivor to initiate a medical malpractice lawsuit.

## Who can Provide Medical Malpractice Insurance in Texas?

In the state of Texas, these entities are authorized to provide medical malpractice insurance.

- Licensed Insurance Companies

- Legislatively created entities like JUA (Texas Medical Liability Insurance Underwriting Association) and Texas Medical Liability Trust (TMLT)

- Medical professional liability funds for state higher education systems

- Surplus lines insurers

- Risk purchasing and risk retention groups

## Types of Medical Malpractice Insurance Coverage in Texas

Medical Professional can get two types of malpractice coverage in Texas categorized into:

1. Occurrence Coverage

Occurrence coverage is a type of medical malpractice insurance coverage that is typically the more expensive option but covers most professionals indefinitely.

It will provide financial coverage (at similar limits) even after the insurance policy is canceled.

Regardless of the time after an incident a claim occurs, professionals are covered even if they no longer have the same malpractice policy.

2. Claims-Made Coverage

Under claims-made malpractice coverage, the health professional is only insured against claims that occur while that policy remains active.

For indefinite coverage, you might have to purchase an extended coverage rider.

Texas legislation generally limits the amount of non-economic damages recoverable to $500,000 in wrongful death claims and $250,000 in other claims.
