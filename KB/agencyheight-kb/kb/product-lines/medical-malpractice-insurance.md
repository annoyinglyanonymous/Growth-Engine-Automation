## Consequences of not Having Medical Malpractice Insurance for Medical Professionals

Practicing without proper medical malpractice insurance—often referred to as “going bare”—creates significant financial, professional, and legal exposure for healthcare providers. Here are the major risks:

#### Financial Exposure and Personal Asset Risk

Malpractice claims often bring significant defense costs, settlements, and court judgments. Without proper coverage, these expenses fall directly on you, putting your savings, property, and long-term financial security at risk.

#### Professional and Career-Limiting Consequences

Many states and healthcare systems require malpractice insurance to practice. Going bare can trigger disciplinary action, license suspension, or even revocation. Hospitals and facilities also demand proof of coverage, limiting your privileges and restricting where and how you can work.

#### Legal and Regulatory Compliance Issues

Several states mandate minimum malpractice insurance for practicing clinicians. Failure to comply can result in fines, penalties, or regulatory review. Operating without coverage may also be seen as neglecting professional standards, damaging employment prospects and patient trust.

Do I need coverage if my employer provides a policy?

Often yes — employer policies rarely include tail coverage or full license protection.

How much does malpractice insurance cost?

Depends on specialty, state, exposure, and policy type. NP costs may start around $700; surgical specialties can reach $20,000+.

Do I need tail coverage?

If you have a claims-made policy and you’re leaving a job or carrier, yes — it’s essential.

What limits should I carry?

Most providers carry $1M/$3M or state-mandated minimums.
