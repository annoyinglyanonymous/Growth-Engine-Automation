## Life Insurance Laws in Texas

Life insurance laws in Texas are governed by the 2005 Texas Insurance Code and Title 28 of Texas Administrative Code. Notable laws regarding life insurance in Texas include:

- A 30-day payment grace period for a Texas policyholder to prevent insurance companies from canceling the policy during that time frame.

- Insurance providers in Texas must pay death benefits to beneficiaries in a timely manner—usually within a month of the claim. If a company takes more than 60 days to process and settle claims, they will be charged accrued interest on the proceeds.

- As an insured, your beneficiaries can claim death benefits, even if the insurance company goes bankrupt. This is backed up by the Texas Life, Accident, Health & Hospital Service Insurance Guarantee Association.

- There are guarantees of up to $300,000 for death benefits that are lost, and $100,000 for lost cash surrender value under the Texas Life, Accident, Health & Hospital Service Insurance Guaranty Association.

- Most of the insurance companies in Texas provide seven to 30 days for a person who has recently bought life insurance. This gives enough time for the insured to make changes if required. If a person cancels their policy within this time period, you can get a full refund of any premiums you have paid so far.

How long does life insurance take to pay out in Texas?

Companies are required to pay death benefits within two months of receiving the death certificate and validating the beneficiary. In the case of an individual life insurance policy, the business is also required to pay interest on the death benefit from the time it received the death certificate until the time it agreed to pay the death benefit.

Does life insurance pay for suicidal death in Texas?

Companies refuse to pay the death benefit in the first two years of a policy if the cause of death is suicide. The premiums must be returned to the beneficiary if the company fails to pay the benefit.

## Florida Life Insurance Laws

Similar to Texas, Florida life insurance laws are regulated by Title 37 of the Florida Statutes. Insurance companies in Florida are also required to have anti-fraud departments. If you’re purchasing life insurance in Florida, here are some things you need to know:

- Florida law requires insurance carriers to find and notify any of the beneficiaries within 120 days of acknowledging the death of the insured. If they aren’t able to locate any of the beneficiaries, then the proceeds go to the Florida state government until the required beneficiary is found.

- A 10-day “free-look” period is provided by most life insurance companies in Florida. This gives the potential policyholder time to cancel their coverage/get a refund of the premiums paid.

- A 30-day payment grace period for a policyholder in Florida ensures that the insurance company does not cancel the policy during that time frame.

- If the death benefits aren’t paid by the company to the beneficiary within 30 days, then the amount goes to accrued interest from the date of death.

- As an insured, your beneficiaries can claim death benefits, even if the insurance company goes bankrupt. This is backed up by the Florida Life & Health Insurance Guaranty Association.

- There will be guarantees of up to $300,000 for death benefits that are lost, and $100,000 for lost cash surrender value under the Florida Life & Health Insurance Guaranty Association.

What is Florida's definition of life insurance replacement?

A contract that increases coverage under existing insurance. A transaction for changing group life insurance into a personal policy is called life insurance replacement in Florida.

Is life insurance taxable in Florida?

No, income taxes are not applied to the sum of money that a life insurance policy provides to a beneficiary.

## Georgia Life Insurance Laws

In Georgia, the life insurance laws are governed by Title 33 of the Georgia Code, which is a part of the 2006 Georgia Code. The laws are quite similar to those in the states mentioned above.

- Under the Georgia Life & Health Insurance Guaranty Association, there are guarantees of up to $300,000 for death benefits that are lost.

- A 30-day payment grace period for a policyholder in Georgia ensures that the insurance company does not cancel the policy during that time frame.

- If the benefits aren’t paid by the company to the beneficiary within 30 days, then the amount goes to accrued interest from the date of death.

- A 10-day “free-look” period is provided by most life insurance companies in Georgia. This gives the potential policyholder time to cancel their coverage/get a refund of the premiums paid.

Do I need life insurance in Georgia?

Even though life insurance is not required in every estate plan, it can be beneficial, particularly for parents of small children and those who support a spouse or an adult or child who is disabled.

What is the minimum age to purchase a life insurance policy in Georgia?

A 15-year-old is able to contract for annuities, endowments, life insurance, and accident and sickness insurance on their own life or body or the life or body of anyone in whom they have an insurable interest in Georgia.

## New York Life Insurance Laws

Life insurance regulations in New York are settled by Article 32 of New York Consolidated Law. The laws, although quite similar to those of Texas, Florida, and Georgia, do have varying conditions when it comes to claim settlements.

- Under the Life Insurance Company Guaranty Corporation of New York, there are guarantees of up to $500,000 for lost death benefits.

- If the death benefits aren’t paid by the company to the beneficiary within 30 days, the amount goes to accrued interest from the date of death.

- A 61-day payment grace period for a policyholder in New York ensures that the insurance company does not cancel the policy during that time frame.

- A 10-day “free-look” period is provided by most life insurance companies in New York. This gives the potential policyholder time to cancel their coverage/get a refund of the premiums paid.
