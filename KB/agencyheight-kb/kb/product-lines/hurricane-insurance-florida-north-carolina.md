Hurricanes Helene and Milton have brought significant attention to the vulnerabilities of homeowners, renters, and insurance markets in North Carolina and Florida. These catastrophic storms have not only caused immense physical destruction but have also exposed critical gaps in hurricane insurance and flood insurance policies, particularly in states prone to such natural disasters.

## Rising Costs of Flood Insurance

In North Carolina, where Hurricane Helene wreaked havoc, many homeowners were shocked to discover that standard home insurance policies do not cover flood damage. For residents of western North Carolina, prior to the hurricanes, purchasing flood insurance wasn’t a strong consideration. But Hurricane Helene flooded most of the area, leaving behind $30 billion in uninsured flood damage.This has led to a surge in demand for supplemental flood insurance policies.

The National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), which provides the bulk of flood coverage in the U.S., faces criticism for outdated risk models and rising premiums. For renters, hurricane coverage is often limited to personal belongings, leaving structural repairs outside their coverage.

Similarly, in Florida, Hurricane Milton underscored the precarious state of the insurance market. Already battling an exodus of private insurers, Florida homeowners have seen skyrocketing premiums for hurricane insurance. Those who managed to secure coverage are still finding gaps in protection, particularly for flood-related damages. These vulnerabilities highlight the urgent need for comprehensive policies that blend home insurance and flood coverage.

## The Role of Climate Change in Insurance and Hurricanes

Climate experts have warned that intensified storm systems like Helene and Milton are becoming more frequent due to rising sea temperatures. This escalation has placed unprecedented stress on insurers in hurricane-prone regions, particularly in Florida and North Carolina. Insurers are raising rates or exiting markets entirely, leaving homeowners and renters scrambling for alternatives.

For renters, hurricane coverage remains a critical but underutilized option. Many renters are unaware that their policies typically exclude flood damage, leaving them vulnerable to waterlogged apartments and uninhabitable homes after storms.

### Flood Insurance Coverage Gaps

In North Carolina, Hurricane Helene revealed that standard homeowners’ insurance policies often exclude flood damage. In Buncombe County, home to Asheville, only 0.9% of homes had flood insurance when the storm hit. This lack of coverage left many homeowners unprotected against the extensive flooding caused by the hurricane.

Similarly, in Florida, Hurricane Milton highlighted deficiencies inflood insuranceuptake. In Sarasota County, which took a direct hit from Milton, just 23% of residents had flood insurance. This low participation rate underscores the widespread exposure to flood risks among Florida homeowners.

### Economic Impact and Insurance Claims

The financial repercussions of these hurricanes are staggering. Hurricane Helene resulted in more than 55,000 National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) claims, with projected losses between $6 billion and $7 billion. Hurricane Milton led to over 18,000 claims, with losses estimated at $1.5 billion to $2.5 billion.

These figures highlight the immense strain on the insurance system and the critical need for adequate coverage.

### Challenges in the Insurance Market

The insurance industry faces significant challenges in the wake of these storms. In Florida, the back-to-back hurricanes have exacerbated an already fragile insurance market, leading to increased premiums and reduced availability of coverage. [Business Insider]

In North Carolina, the realization that standard homeowners’ policies do not cover flood damage has prompted a reevaluation of insurance needs among residents.

## Closing the Coverage Gap

To mitigate future risks, it is imperative for homeowners and renters in hurricane-prone areas to assess their insurance policies comprehensively. Understanding the limitations of standard coverage and considering additional flood insurance can provide essential protection against the financial devastation of hurricanes. Renters should also ensure their policies include provisions for hurricane-related damage to personal property.

To mitigate the financial impact of hurricanes, both states must address the gaps in hurricane insurance. Florida, with its history of insurance crises, requires reforms to stabilize its home insurance market. Simultaneously, North Carolina must encourage more private flood insurance providers to reduce dependency on the NFIP.

For homeowners, combining hurricane insurance and flood insurance is becoming a necessity, not a choice. Renters, too, should seek policies that explicitly include hurricane coverage for personal property.

## Conclusion

Hurricanes Helene and Milton have underscored the critical importance of comprehensive flood insurance coverage in North Carolina and Florida.
