## Types of Trucking Insurance Coverage

Trucking insurance is anything but standardized. Depending on how you operate your trucking business, what type of cargo you haul, and what your needs truly are, you may need a combination of the following coverage:

#### Primary Liability Insurance

What It Covers: Any damages or injuries you cause to others in the event of an accident.

Why You Need It: This is the most basic legally required coverage for all truckers. For most carriers, FMCSA mandates liability limits of $750,000, which can vary based on type of freight transported.

#### Physical Damage Insurance

What It Covers: Repairs to or replacement of your truck if damaged in an accident, theft, fire, vandalism, or other natural disasters.

Why You Need It: Your truck is your most valuable asset. This insurance saves you from huge out-of-pocket expenses needed to get back on your road.

#### Cargo Insurance

What It Covers: Damage, loss, or theft of the goods being transported.

Why You Need It: This insurance is non-negotiable if you are hauling high-value or sensitive cargo; some shippers might require it just to do business with you.

#### Non-Trucking Liability Insurance

What It Covers: Liability arises when the operator and the truck are engaged in personal use, such as for errands or commuting.

Why You Need It: The motor carrier’s insurance may not cover an owner-operator for various activities outside of business hours.

#### Trailer Interchange Insurance

What It Covers: Covers trailer damages trailers that are not yours but are being used under a trailer interchange agreement.

Why You Need It: If you often interchange trailers with others, this insurance protects you for damage to another’s trailer.

#### Motor Truck Cargo Insurance

What It Covers: Special coverage for high-value, hazardous, or perishable goods.

Why You Need It: Unique shipments may require insurance that standard cargo insurance might not fully cover.

#### General Liability Insurance

What It Covers: Risks unrelated to driving, such as injuries occurring at your place of business or to a client’s property.

Why You Need It: It is a good addition for trucking companies that have offices, warehouses, or other physical locations.

## How Much Does Trucking Insurance Cost?

Trucking insurance costs can greatly vary with many factors. On average, an independent truck driver is likely to spend anywhere from $12,000 to $15,000 per year on minimum coverage while larger fleets tend to pay much more.

### Other Limitations Affecting the Price

Driving History: If you have a clean driving record, you may get lower premiums; on the other hand, any accidents or other violations will raise your premiums.

Type of Cargo: Transporting hazardous materials or high-value items has Insurance premiums which are considerably above average.

Mileage: Wherever there are miles put on the truck, there will definitely be some risk factors introduced as well: thus, higher and higher premiums.

Location: Costs will possibly rise for trucks operating in congested areas or particularly adverse weather condition-prone areas.

Deductibles: Higher the deductible, less the premium but for claims, you would be paying more from your pocket.

## Legal Requirements for Trucking Insurance in the U.S.

The FMCSA sets down the minimum trucking insurance requirements, but individual states may establish additional regulations. Here’s what you need to know:

#### Federal Requirements

Primary Liability Insurance: Minimum coverage of $750,000 for most freight carriers. Moreover, for hazardous material, the limit increases to $1,000,000 or $5,000,000, based on the type of cargo.

Proof of Insurance: You must always carry proof of insurance (Form MCS-90) in your truck.

#### State-Specific Regulations

A few states require further endorsements or carry higher coverage limits. For example:

California: The state of California requires higher liability limits for certain freight classifications.

Texas: The state has laws specifically for intrastate carriers (those operating solely within the state).

How does truck insurance work?

Commercial trucking coverage protects trucking drivers and trucking businesses from financial losses due to unexpected accidents, damage to cargo, or liability incurred through different types of coverage like liability insurance, cargo insurance, physical damage insurance, non-trucking liability, workers compensation, uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage etc.

Can I operate without trucking insurance?

No. Trucking insurance is legally required for all commercial drivers across the United States. Operating without it can result in fines, license suspension, or even criminal charges

What happens if my insurance lapses?

Delays in securing coverage can lead to serious consequences, including fines, loss of your CDL, and difficulty finding affordable insurance in the future. Always renew your policy on time.
