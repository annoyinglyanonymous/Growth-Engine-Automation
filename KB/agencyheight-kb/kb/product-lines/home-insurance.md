## What Homeowners Insurance Covers

A standard homeowners insurance policy protects your home, belongings, and liability—so you can recover quickly from unexpected events.

#### Dwelling Coverage

Protects your home’s structure—walls, roof, foundation, and built-in appliances.

#### Other Structures

Covers detached structures like garages, sheds, and fences (usually 10% of dwelling coverage).

#### Personal Property Coverage

Replaces or repairs belongings like furniture, electronics, and clothing.

#### Personal Liability Coverage

Protects you if someone is injured on your property or you accidentally damage someone else’s property.

#### Loss of Use / Additional Living Expenses

Pays for temporary housing and meals if your home is uninhabitable after a covered event.

## How Much Does Homeowners Insurance Cost?

Most homeowners pay around $2,100 per year for a standard policy, but your cost can be higher or lower depending on several key factors.

State-Wise Rates

- Florida: ~$5,640/year – high hurricane risk.

- Louisiana: ~$5,136/year – hurricane and flood exposure.

- Nebraska: ~$5,912/year – tornado-prone regions.

- Hawaii: ~$616–$733/year – low natural disaster risk.

- Vermont: ~$816/year – among the lowest in the nation.

## What Affects Your Premium?

###### Location

Homes in areas prone to hurricanes, wildfires, floods, or other natural hazards cost more to insure.

###### Age of the home

Older homes or those with outdated wiring, plumbing, or roofing cost more to insure.

###### Claims history

Recent insurance claims or lower credit scores can increase rates.

###### Natural disaster

Some insurance companies will give you a discount if you buy multiple coverages through them.
