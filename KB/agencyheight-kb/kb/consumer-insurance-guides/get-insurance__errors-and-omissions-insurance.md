Is E & O Insurance required by law?

No, E&O insurance is not mandatory by law in all fifty states. Certain states have set federal requirements on E&O insurance for professions that involve higher risks like law practice and medical field.

For instance, in the state of Tennessee, timeshare salesperson, affiliate brokers and real estate brokers are required by law to have errors and omissions insurance. Similarly, Colorado mandates E&O coverage for all real estate businesses.

Even in the states where it is not mandated, businesses are advised to purchase E&O insurance to protect themselves from the financial damages that can result from errors and omissions.

When should I purchase E&O insurance?

You should purchase E&O insurance as soon as you start your business. This way, you will be protected from the financial damage that will result from errors and omissions.

What does E&O insurance cover?

Errors and omissions insurance policies cover attorney fees, court costs, and settlements costs up to the policy’s limits. This also includes a variety of claims, including negligence, misrepresentation, breach of contract, and even unfair competition. Some policies also provide coverage for pre-judgment interest and punitive damages, which are not typically covered by general liability insurance.

However, the forms of insurance coverage that are most appropriate for you will be determined by a variety of circumstances, including the following:

- Age

- Driving records

- Condition of your vehicles

- Use of your trucks and cargo

- State and federal requirements

- Distance travelled

## Are Errors and Omissions Insurance, Professional Liability Insurance, and Malpractice Insurance the Same?

One can often get confused with terminologies in the world of insurance. Professional liability insurance, malpractice insurance, and E & O insurance are terms that can be used interchangeably in most situations. The usage of these words generally differs according to the industry that you’re working in. However, the purpose of the insurance is the same, no matter the terminology.
