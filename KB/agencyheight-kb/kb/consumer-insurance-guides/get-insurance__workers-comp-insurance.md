Workers comp insurance is an essential way of protecting your employees and your business. Every business with employees should have workers compensation insurance, as it covers medical expenses and lost wages that could result from a workplace injury or illness. It also offers protection against lawsuits related to those injuries or illnesses.

Not only does workers compensation provide peace of mind for employers, but it also gives them the financial resources they need in the event of a workplace accident or illness. With the right coverage in place, stay assured knowing that your staff are protected, and your business is covered.

A worker comp insurance checklist is an important tool for employers to ensure they are properly covering their workers and protecting them from potential losses. This checklist will help employers identify areas where they may need additional coverage so they can make sure their employee’s safety is taken care of and protected.

## How Much Does Workers Compensation Insurance Cost?

Understanding workers’ compensation insurance costs is essential for making sure you are protecting your business and employees. This will help you locate savings options and make sure that your premiums meet the standard industry rate.

On average, American businesses pay $936 annually per employee for Workers’ Compensation Insurance. However, the actual premium rate is dependent on a variety of factors such as state regulations, industry type, years served and any claims filed against the company. In total this expenditure amounts to 1.2% of an employee’s salary according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data; that equates to around $78 every month.

What is Workers' Compensation?

Workers Compensation Insurance provides health and wage benefits to people who are injured or sick on the job. Workers’ remuneration is considered social insurance because it is based on a social contract between the employer and the employee.

How do I pay for my Workers Compensation policy?

Your employer pays for workers’ compensation insurance, regardless of which state you are in. An employee’s compensation cost is a percentage of their payroll.
