A recreational vehicle, or RV, can serve as a temporary habitation and a permanent home. In either case, it is a priceless possession that requires insurance. Because it serves to protect you and the vehicle in the event of a collision, theft, or breakdown, having an RV insurance policy is crucial.

The best RV insurance cost each year might range from $200 to $3000, depending on how often you use it. Full-timers may earn up to $2,000 to $3,000 while part-timers typically earn less than $1,000.

This works out to about $1,500 a year on average, but as there are many factors to consider when figuring up rates and discounts, your insurance cost may be higher or lower than $1,500.

## What Factors Affect The Price of RV insurance?

Similar to car insurance, RV coverage costs vary according to the vehicle’s age, size, and driving history.

### Do RV class affect RV insurance cost?

Usually, the cost of insurance increases as an RV gets bigger or more expensive. RVs are divided into three classes by insurers: Class A, B, and C.

Class A: These are the largest, most opulent motorhomes resembling buses. They are typically the most expensive to insure, expensive, and can fit up to 10 people. They also run-on gas or diesel.

Class B: Camper and sleeper vans are under the Class B category of recreational vehicles. They have fewer amenities such as kitchens, beds, and bathrooms than Class A vehicles but are more fuel-efficient. The least expensive of the three classifications are frequently camper insurance.

Class C: A truck or van base with an aloft sleeping area characterizes these mid-sized travel trailers. They have a towing capacity, cost less to acquire and insure than Class A vehicles, but are larger than Class B vehicles.

Additionally, travel trailers that are hitched or being towed might be covered by RV insurance. These typically cost less to insure and include pop-up tents and fifth wheels.

### Do RV specs affect RV insurance?

Your RV’s features, model, mileage, and age can affect the price of your RV insurance.

For instance, some amenities, like slide-outs—extended living areas in Class A motorhomes—may boost your premium because they can increase the likelihood of mechanical repairs or tire blowouts.

Newer model RVs may carry less risk because they have warranties and are less prone to problems. However, older ones with more wear and tear and high mileage may cost more.

High mileage, particularly on a newer car, may indicate that you travel more frequently and pose a liability concern. In addition, a vehicle with a history of high mileage may experience more breakdowns.

## Common Discounts You May Find for Your RV Insurance

Discounts may significantly affect the cost of your RV policy. There are both general discounts offered by insurance carriers and discounts unique to insurers. Here are some things to consider while searching for quotes:

Bundling: If you combine several insurance policies—car insurance, home insurance, boat insurance—into one, the insurers will lower your premiums.

Good driver: Your premium will often be lower if you have a clean driving record with no incidents or moving offenses in the last three years.

Original owner: First-time RV or travel trailer owners may occasionally qualify for an insurance reduction.

No smoking: Some insurers offer discounts to consumers who choose not to smoke, so you might have to inquire about this.

Paperless: Sign up for paperless billing and bills for a bonus discount.

Homeowner: Obtain a discount by merely being a homeowner. Mobile homes, condos, and duplexes are all eligible for the discount.

Pay-in-full: If you pay for your annual policy all at once instead of breaking it up into monthly payments, many carriers may give you a discount.

Safety features: Options like airbags, anti-lock brakes, and anti-theft systems may result in significant savings.

Storage option: When your RV is stored for more than 30 days, you may be able to save money by suspending some of your coverage.

Military or professional: Additional savings may be available for service members, educators, government employees, and healthcare professionals.
