## What Does Commercial Auto Insurance Cover?

Commercial auto insurance coverage includes the following coverage options:

Collision Coverage

Collision coverage covers accidental damage to your insured business vehicle. It reimburses your expenses related to vehicle repairs and even helps to replace your vehicle in case of total damage.

Comprehensive Coverage

Comprehensive coverage shields your business from financial burdens resulting from non-collision related damage like vandalism, theft, and extreme weather conditions.

Underinsured/Uninsured Motorist Coverage

Underinsured/Uninsured motorist coverage covers the medical bills if you or one of your employees meets with an accident and an uninsured or underinsured motorist is at fault.

Liability Coverage

Liability coverage generally includes two types – bodily injury coverage and property damage coverage. Bodily injury coverage covers the medical expenses of the third-party if you are held liable in an accident. Similarly, property damage covers the vehicle damage expenses and property damage costs of the other party if you are at fault. Furthermore, this coverage can cover your legal expenses as well.

Rental Reimbursement Coverage

Rental reimbursement temporarily covers the expenses for a rental vehicle if your insured business vehicle gets damaged in an accident and needs repair.

There are the normal coverage options included in a standard business auto policy. Business owners can also include additional coverage in their policy to protect the contents inside a vehicle as well.

## Difference Between Commercial Auto Insurance and Personal Auto Insurance

Understanding the differences between commercial auto insurance and personal auto insurance is pivotal for business owners as the line between a personal vehicle and a commercial vehicle can often seem blurry.

Many small businesses use their personal vehicles for commercial purposes like delivery, and commuting to work . So, does this make commercial auto insurance redundant for such businesses? Absolutely not.

Even if your vehicle has personal auto insurance, it will not cover losses incurred during commercial activities. A personal auto insurance policy is only designed to cover personal driving. Hence, if you use your personal vehicle primarily for commercial purposes, your personal auto insurance will not be obliged to reimburse your expenses.

That is why every business needs to invest in the right business auto insurance coverage. If you and your employees use personal vehicles for some commercial tasks, you should consider getting a Hired and Non-Owned Auto Insurance (HNOA) policy.

Also, commercial automobile insurance is generally more expensive than personal auto insurance as commercial vehicles involve a higher degree of risk than private vehicles.
