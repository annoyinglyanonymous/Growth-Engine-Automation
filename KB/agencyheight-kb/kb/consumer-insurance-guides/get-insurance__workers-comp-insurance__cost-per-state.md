Workers’ Compensation Insurance, also known as Workman’s Compensation Insurance, is a government-mandated insurance program that provides employees with benefits if they are injured as a result of work-related injury or illness with medical, disability, and rehabilitation insurance.

In almost all states, businesses are required by law to have workers’ compensation insurance, with a few exceptions. Let’s have a look into worker’s compensation insurance by state.

## Workers Comp Insurance in New York

New York usually jumps between first and second place on the list of the most expensive workers’ compensation insurance plans in the country. Currently, he ranks second only to New Jersey, with a premium index rate averaging $2.23 per $100 salary.

Most states adhere to the National Compensation Insurance Council (NCCI) and its data classification and Experience Modification Rate (EMR). However, in New York, rates are calculated based on several factors that vary from company to company and are regulated by the New York Compensation Insurance Rating Board (NYCIRB).

## Workers Comp Texas

Texas law is different, employers are not required to have employee compensation insurance. Limited exceptions to this rule may include:

- Companies that enter into building or construction contracts with government agencies

- Public or educational employers

- Independent contractors may not have employer insurance and may be required to provide proof of employment

As per NCCI that average rate per $100 of payroll has fallen from his $2.32 in 2003 to $0.86 in 2015.

Declines in claims and injuries show that jobs in Texas is a safe state, and lower premium rates are encouraging employers to purchase workers’ compensation insurance.
