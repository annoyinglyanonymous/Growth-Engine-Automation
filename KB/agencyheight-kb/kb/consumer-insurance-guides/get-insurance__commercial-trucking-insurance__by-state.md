## Commercial Truck Insurance Requirements in Florida

The following coverages are required by Florida’s state laws for commercial trucking businesses.

1. Minimum Liability Coverage

Trucking businesses should have a minimum liability coverage of amounts ranging from $250,000 to $5 million. The coverage breakdown is provided as follows:

General Freight: $750,000

Household Items: $300,000

Oil Transport: $1 million

Hazardous Materials (Hazmat): $5 million

2. Federal Liability Coverage

The truck drivers must meet the FMCSA federal liability limits that vary between $750,000 to $1 million, if they are traveling across state-lines.

3. Personal Injury Protection

Legal entities also advise truckers to obtain personal injury protection to cover injury-related expenses that happen due to trucking accidents. This can cover medical bills, loss of income due to disability, and household services to the truck drivers.

4. Bodily Injury Liability

This liability coverage provides for any serious injury or death caused to someone by a truck driver. If a case is filed against the trucking business, this can cover up for the legal representation of the truck driver or the business itself.

Besides these, interstate truckers also require the following coverage:

Cargo Insurance

Bobtail Insurance

Physical Damage Insurance

### Legal and Document Requirements for Commercial Trucking Businesses in Florida

Florida state laws require all commercial trucking businesses to have commercial trucking insurance. Along with the general commercial trucking insurance requirements as mandated by Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMSCA), the trucking businesses also get specific insurance requirements according to the type of cargo the truck is hauling.

Trucking companies need to display a valid USDOT number or a Florida state registration number. They also need to register with the Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT), and comply with all the FDOT truck insurance requirements that are laid out by FMSCA.

Additionally, the FMSCA and Florida state laws require some additional documents to be submitted by an insurance agent or company. Some of the forms that are required are:

- Form E

- Form H

- BMC-92

- Form MCS-91

## Commercial Truck Insurance Requirements in Texas

Texas trucking insurance law mandates that new businesses and local truckers have a specific level of liability insurance. The kind of freight you pull and the distance you haul it decide how much coverage you need. You must obtain a TXDMV number and maintain specific liability insurance if you don’t cross state boundaries and your truck weighs more than GVW. Minimum amount of liability insurance being:

- Household Goods: $300,000

- General Freight: $500,000

- Oil Transport: $1 million

- Hazmat: $5 million

- Cargo Insurance (Intrastate household goods movers and interstate contract carriers): $5,000/vehicle and $10,000/catastrophe

You must also adhere to all federally mandated regulations for semi-truck insurance in addition to those mentioned above. Federal liability caps often range between $750,000 and $1,000,000.

Your insurance agent must submit specific filings required by Texas and the FMCSA. These filings should be handled for you by your insurance agent or insurance provider. These forms demonstrate to the government that all your DOT truck insurance requirements are satisfied. Before you may be given your authority, they must be presented. Some common forms include:

- Form MCS-98

- Form H

- Form E

- The BMC-99

## Commercial Truck Insurance Requirements in California

The insurance requirements for commercial trucks in California vary depending on the freight carried. Every commercial truck driver operating solely within California state lines in a vehicle that is more than 10,000 gross vehicle weight (GVW) must have an intrastate identification number as well as liability insurance depending on the freight:

- Oil Transport: $1 million

- Hazmat: $5 million

- General Freight: $750,000

- Household Goods: $300,000

- Cargo Insurance: $20,000 per vehicle and $20,000 per catastrophe
